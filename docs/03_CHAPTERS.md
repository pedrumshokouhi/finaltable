# Final Table — Campaign Content Specification (10 Chapters × 10 Levels)

Each level entry: **ID (difficulty) — Title — scenario/objective brief.** Full JSON per `02_SCHEMAS.md` is authored in Phases 5–6; ten complete examples are in `04_SAMPLE_SCENARIOS.md`. Difficulty bands are validator-enforced per chapter.

**Component activation defaults** (C1/C2): Ch1 = decision+variables+confidence; Ch2+ adds probability; sizing activates in Ch5 and appears selectively afterward.

---

## Chapter 1 — Foundations (D1–2) · skill: hand recognition
Primer: ranks, the eight-plus-one hand categories, reading five shared cards, best-five selection, ties.

- **L001 (1) — Reading Your Hand** — Identify a simple pair on a five-card board; match a tiny requirement with the best hand possible.
- **L002 (1) — The Best Five** — Seven cards available, only five count; spot that your low pair doesn't play.
- **L003 (1) — Category Ladder** — Straight vs. flush showdown read; withdraw the loser despite it "looking pretty."
- **L004 (1) — The Shared Trap** — The board itself is the best hand; recognize a certain tie and pass rather than pay.
- **L005 (2) — Two Pair, Not Three** — A-9 on A-9-K-K-x: two pair aces-and-kings, nine kicker — the three-pair illusion (sample scenario S1).
- **L006 (2) — Kicker Decides** — Both players pair the ace; walk the kicker comparison before choosing match vs. withdraw.
- **L007 (2) — Counterfeited** — Your small two pair is erased when the board pairs higher; downgrade honestly.
- **L008 (2) — Hidden Straight** — Spot a five-card straight using one hole card; challenge with confidence.
- **L009 (2) — The Tie You Missed** — Identical best-fives from different hole cards; identify the split before acting.
- **L010 (2) — Foundations Exam** — Mixed board read with all prior traps; certificate level.

## Chapter 2 — Probability (D2–4) · skill: probability
Primer: counting helpful cards, exact vs. friendly estimates (×4/×2 rule), clean vs. tainted outs, misconceptions (gambler's fallacy, "due" cards).

- **L011 (2) — Counting Helpers** — Four-to-a-flush after three shared cards; count 9 helpful cards, estimate, then match a cheap requirement.
- **L012 (2) — One Card Left** — Same draw, one street: 9/46 ≈ 19.6% — feel the drop from two streets.
- **L013 (3) — The Rule of Four** — Practice ×4 approximation vs. exact 34.97%; score is on the estimate (sample scenario S2).
- **L014 (3) — Open on Both Ends** — 8-out straight draw (31.5%); compare price to probability and match.
- **L015 (3) — Tainted Outs** — Straight-draw cards that also complete an opponent's likely flush; discount and withdraw.
- **L016 (3) — Long Shots Cost More** — 2-out set chase (8.4%) against a big requirement; withdraw and bank the lesson.
- **L017 (3) — Never 'Due'** — Identical draw missed twice in the timeline; probability is memoryless — estimate unchanged.
- **L018 (4) — Combined Chances** — Flush + straight combo draw (15 clean outs, 54.1%); challenge is now excellent.
- **L019 (4) — Price vs. Chance** — Two puzzles in one sitting: same 35% draw, good price then bad price; opposite answers.
- **L020 (4) — Probability Exam** — Estimate within ±3 pp under a messy board; certificate level.

## Chapter 3 — Position (D3–5) · skill: position
Primer: early/middle/late/final; acting last = free information; players-behind risk.

- **L021 (3) — Last Is Best** — Identical hand shown twice (early vs. final seat); choose differently and see why.
- **L022 (3) — First Is Blind** — Early seat, medium hand, five behind; pass/withdraw discipline.
- **L023 (4) — Information Buys Points** — Final seat after two passes; challenge medium strength into shown weakness.
- **L024 (4) — The Squeeze Seat** — Middle seat between challenger and unknowns; strong-selection standard.
- **L025 (4) — Late Steal Logic** — K9 in final position, all passed; a modest challenge is excellent (sample scenario S3).
- **L026 (4) — Defending the Commitment** — Your forced commitment is under challenge; price + position math.
- **L027 (4) — The Late Seat Advantage** — Draw + last action vs. aggressive challenger (schema example level).
- **L028 (5) — Fewer Players, Wider Hands** — 3-handed final seat; re-rank the same starting class.
- **L029 (5) — Out of Position After** — Winning the round now but acting first on every later street; size down.
- **L030 (5) — Position Exam** — Same cards, three seats, three answers; certificate level.

## Chapter 4 — Resource Depth (D4–6) · skill: resource management
Primer: stack-to-requirement ratio, commitment thresholds, preserving optionality, short/medium/deep play.

- **L031 (4) — Depth Changes Everything** — Same premium pair at 8× vs. 60× requirement; different lines.
- **L032 (4) — The Commitment Point** — Already ⅓ committed; finish what the math started.
- **L033 (5) — Short Means Simple** — 7× stack, strong ace: full commitment beats a fancy small challenge (sample scenario S4).
- **L034 (5) — Deep Means Careful** — 80× deep, one pair; avoid stacking off — optionality has value.
- **L035 (5) — Don't Cripple Yourself** — Match would leave a useless 2× remainder; withdraw or commit, never dribble.
- **L036 (5) — Covering vs. Covered** — Same hand vs. bigger and smaller stacks; risk asymmetry.
- **L037 (5) — Implied Later Streets** — Deep stacks make a small set-chase profitable; revisit L016 with new depth.
- **L038 (6) — The Awkward Middle** — 18× stack: too deep to shove, too short to speculate; tight-aggressive resolution.
- **L039 (6) — Optionality Priced In** — Fold a decent hand because depth multiplies future difficult spots out of position.
- **L040 (6) — Depth Exam** — Three stack depths, one decision each; certificate level.

## Chapter 5 — Action Sizing (D4–6) · skill: action sizing (sizing component activates)
Primer: size as a message and a price; proportional thinking; small/medium/large jobs.

- **L041 (4) — The Slider Awakens** — First sized challenge: pick ½-pool for value with top pair.
- **L042 (5) — Small Sizes, Big Boards** — Static board: ⅓-pool does the whole job cheaper.
- **L043 (5) — Big Sizes, Wet Boards** — Draw-heavy board: charge ¾+ with a strong made hand (sample scenario S5).
- **L044 (5) — Pricing Out the Draw** — Choose the smallest size that makes a 35% draw's match clearly bad.
- **L045 (5) — The Too-Small Tell** — Tiny challenge with a monster invites three opponents; size up.
- **L046 (5) — The Too-Big Leak** — Overpaying to "protect" against a 5% threat; efficiency scoring bites.
- **L047 (6) — Proportional, Not Absolute** — Same 400-pt challenge is huge in one pool, trivial in another.
- **L048 (6) — Sizing Down in Position** — Last action lets a smaller size achieve the same fold-out.
- **L049 (6) — The All-In Threshold** — When stack ≤ 1.4× pool, the only honest sizes are all or nothing.
- **L050 (6) — Sizing Exam** — Three spots, three bands; certificate level.

## Chapter 6 — Opponent Profiles (D5–7) · skill: opponent modeling
Primer: five profiles — cautious, balanced, aggressive, unpredictable, overly passive — and what their actions *mean*.

- **L051 (5) — The Cautious Tell** — A cautious profile's sudden big challenge = strength; withdraw a good hand.
- **L052 (5) — Passive Means Weak(er)** — Overly-passive opponent finally challenges tiny; match light, but don't raise.
- **L053 (6) — Aggro Pays You Off** — Value-challenge thinner vs. aggressive matcher.
- **L054 (6) — Calling the Bluffer** — Bluff-catch K-Q high pair vs. aggressive triple-barrel (sample scenario S6).
- **L055 (6) — Unpredictable = Tighten Up** — Vs. noise, stop reading and revert to baseline math.
- **L056 (6) — Same Action, Two Meanings** — Identical challenge from cautious vs. aggressive; opposite responses.
- **L057 (6) — Exploiting the Rock Garden** — Two cautious opponents; challenge more boards with air.
- **L058 (7) — Don't Bluff the Station** — Overly passive opponents never withdraw; value only.
- **L059 (7) — Profile Drift** — Timeline shows a cautious player acting out of character; weigh recency vs. baseline (labeled behavioral assumption).
- **L060 (7) — Profiles Exam** — Four seats, four profiles, one decision; certificate level.

## Chapter 7 — Multi-Player Decisions (D6–8) · skill: multi-player reasoning
Primer: more players = stronger standards; shared-card coordination; who a size targets.

- **L061 (6) — Standards Rise** — Top pair, fine heads-up, is a withdraw four-way.
- **L062 (6) — Someone Has It** — Coordinated board + action from two seats: beat-the-field math.
- **L063 (7) — Three-Way Squeeze** — Challenge + match ahead of you; the matcher's range matters too.
- **L064 (7) — Top Pair, Traffic Ahead** — K-T top pair into challenge-and-match; disciplined withdraw (sample scenario S7).
- **L065 (7) — Draws Love Company** — Multiway improves draw prices; match where heads-up you'd withdraw.
- **L066 (7) — Isolate the Weak Seat** — Size a challenge to fold two and keep one passive matcher.
- **L067 (7) — The Family Pool** — Five-way pool: nut-or-nothing selection standard.
- **L068 (8) — Split Attention** — Two textures favoring different seats; assign ranges around the table.
- **L069 (8) — Last In, Best Priced** — Closing the action multiway with a marginal hand; price-only match.
- **L070 (8) — Multiway Exam** — Four-way with sizing decision; certificate level.

## Chapter 8 — Tournament Pressure (D7–9) · skill: tournament pressure (Standing Value Model activates, C10)
Primer: ranking points, survival value, reward jumps, big-stack leverage, short-stack urgency — all fictional points/badges.

- **L071 (7) — Points Aren't Linear** — First standing-value puzzle: a 50/50 for your whole stack is worse than it looks.
- **L072 (7) — The Reward Jump** — One elimination from a badge tier; tighten dramatically.
- **L073 (8) — Big Stack Bullying (You)** — Facing the leader's pressure near a jump; withdraw a strong hand — and see the standing math (sample scenario S8).
- **L074 (8) — Big Stack Bullying (As You)** — You're the leader; challenge relentlessly into medium stacks who can't fight back.
- **L075 (8) — Short Stack Urgency** — 6× stack, decent ace: commitment now beats blinding down.
- **L076 (8) — The Middle Squeeze** — Medium stack between a desperate short and a bullying leader; the worst seat in the game.
- **L077 (8) — Ladder or Attack?** — Guaranteed climb by passing vs. profitable-but-risky challenge; standing value decides.
- **L078 (9) — Two Short Stacks** — Let them fight: withdraw hands you'd normally play; survival value quantified.
- **L079 (9) — Pressure + Position** — Leader on your right vs. on your left; same cards, different answers.
- **L080 (9) — Pressure Exam** — Full standing-value decision with sizing; certificate level.

## Chapter 9 — Advanced Reasoning (D8–10) · skill: (multi) probability + opponent modeling
Primer: sequences tell stories; category-range thinking; card removal ("blockers"); polarized actions; delayed challenges; mixed decisions.

- **L081 (8) — The Story So Far** — Read a three-street sequence; the line only makes sense one way.
- **L082 (8) — Ranges, Not Hands** — Score is on assigning the challenger's plausible categories, then deciding.
- **L083 (8) — The Card You Hold** — Your ace of spades removes the top flush; matching down becomes right.
- **L084 (9) — Removal as a Weapon** — Holding the key blocker, a polarized re-challenge bluff is excellent (sample scenario S9).
- **L085 (9) — Polar or Merged?** — Classify an opponent's size as polarized vs. merged; respond accordingly.
- **L086 (9) — The Delayed Challenge** — Pass a strong hand now to challenge bigger later; sequence planning.
- **L087 (9) — The Impossible Hand** — Timeline + your cards make their claimed monster nearly combinatorially impossible.
- **L088 (9) — Mixed by Design** — Two options tie at Excellent; learn why balance itself is the answer.
- **L089 (10) — Reverse Read** — What does *your* line look like to them? Exploit your own table image.
- **L090 (10) — Reasoning Exam** — Blockers + ranges + sequence in one puzzle; certificate level.
